## Step 0 — where the phase lives, and why the fix is a FIXTURE constant, not an engine change

Read before touching anything (step 2's "find the data, not the constant"):

- Canon's clock: `eGFXAnimStates[0]` (`0x020094c0`) hw1 is a frame countdown; `ProcessGFXAnims`
  decrements it and reloads it from the entry's hold field (T23's table, asm00_0.c:2359-2394).
  At the field row's paired anchor it reads **4** — canon is 4 frames into an 8-frame hold
  (reload to 8 at caps 139/147/... , `/tmp/bn-t23-art-clock-phase/canon_animstates.bin`).
- Our side: `FIELD_ZERO art_entry=10, art_timer=7` (tools/harness.py:1564-1565) →
  `Backdrop::seed`'s `self.timer = timer + 1` (src/backdrop.rs:233) → timer 8 at the anchor.
  `update()` (src/backdrop.rs:265-272) decrements and fires `show_step` **same-frame** when it
  hits 0; canon's copy goes through `QueueEightWordAlignedGFXTransfer` and lands one frame after
  its clock edge (src/backdrop.rs:225-231's own note).
- **Is 4 derivable from data?** No. Canon's 4 is "4 frames into entry 24's 8-frame hold at the
  anchor" — a property of where `/tmp/pausedwithcannon.state` sits in the 192-frame supercycle.
  `STEP_HOLD` (src/backdrop.rs:90-92) encodes per-entry holds, not within-entry phase; no table
  in our tree or in canon's dump encodes "the anchor is 4 frames into its entry". So a
  data-driven seed (timer from `STEP_HOLD[entry]`) has no source for this number and would also
  silently change WARP/BUSTER/CHIPUSE (whose `art_timer` values 6/0/3 are *not* their holds).
- **Fixture or engine?** The engine's seed machinery (construction lead +1) is the documented
  pipeline compensation and is correct; only the fixture's phase constant is stale (off by 4).
  The honest fix belongs to the **fixture**: `FIELD_ZERO art_timer` only. This also means the
  guard rows (`tiles`/`gauge`/`opening`/`mettaur`/`result`/`windowclose`/`chip-use`/`cursor`/
  `gunner`) use *other* fixtures (`_tiles_gauge`, `OPEN_ROW`, `WARP_ZERO`, ...) and are
  structurally unreachable by this change — they are still measured below, because the ROM
  bytes change and the ticket demands it.

**Which value.** The judged pixels are canon's *resident* VRAM, which appears one frame AFTER
canon's clock edge. Our port writes same-frame. Current seed `timer=8` fires our upload 3 frames
after canon's resident edge (F47's +3). Aligning resident art means firing at canon's
clock-edge+1: timer 8 → **5**, i.e. `art_timer 7 → 4`. T23's naive `7 → 3` (timer 4 = canon's
literal countdown) would fire at the clock edge itself — resident one frame EARLY, which is
exactly the recorded 208-px-at-k=7 failure of the bare peeked Timer 4 (src/backdrop.rs:225-231).
The ticket's own `4 = 3 + 1` decomposition says the same: clock offset 4, resident offset 3,
difference = the pipeline frame. Pixels arbitrate below.

per-frame average of field-bg1's residue (~8862 px) exceeds a full art-step difference (~2368 px),

T23's naive `art_timer 3` was not run (budget); both the pipeline argument and the recorded 208-px
failure of the bare Timer 4 predict it is one misaligned frame in 8 WORSE than the landed 4.

Branch disposition: the change is a strict improvement on the judged lines with zero regression

merges or the branch waits for a wider re-derivation of the residue. Per the ticket, if any zero
row had moved this would be a hard stop -- none did.
